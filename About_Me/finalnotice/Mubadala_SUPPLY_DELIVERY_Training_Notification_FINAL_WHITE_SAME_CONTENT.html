⚠ Tips to identify phishing messages
✔ Pause before clicking. Unexpected supplier, procurement, delivery or tracking requests deserve extra attention, especially when the message creates urgency.
✔ Verify the supplier independently. Use a known internal contact or an approved supplier relationship to confirm the request rather than relying on the message itself.
✔ Inspect the destination. Check where a link leads before opening it. A familiar delivery or tracking theme does not make the destination trustworthy.
✔ Be careful with attachments. Unexpected delivery notes, invoices, purchase documents or shipping attachments should be verified before opening.
✔ Watch for urgency and changed details. Requests to act immediately, update information or follow a new process can be signs of a phishing attempt.
✔ Report suspicious messages. Use the organization's approved Outlook reporting process or contact the Information Security team when a supplier or delivery message does not look right.
