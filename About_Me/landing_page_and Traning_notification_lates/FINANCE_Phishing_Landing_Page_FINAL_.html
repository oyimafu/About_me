What should you have noticed?

Finance-themed phishing attacks often rely on urgency, authority and requests involving money or account information. Slow down and verify before acting.

01
Be suspicious of urgency around money. Pressure such as “by end of today”, payment delays or pending verification can be used to make you act before thinking.
02
Don't use email links for sensitive actions. Access expense, reimbursement or payment systems through known bookmarks or your normal internal process.
03
Verify financial requests independently. For unexpected payment, expense or reimbursement requests, contact the relevant team using a known internal channel.
04
Check where a link really goes. Hover over links and inspect the destination. A familiar-looking message can still point to an unrelated domain.
05
Report suspicious messages. Use your organization's reporting process or the Report Message function in Outlook so the security team can investigate.
06
Pause when something feels unusual. Unexpected requests, unusual wording, unfamiliar senders or changes to normal payment procedures deserve a second look.
