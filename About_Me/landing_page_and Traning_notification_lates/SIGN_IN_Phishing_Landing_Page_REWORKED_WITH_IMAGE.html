What should you have noticed in this sign-in alert?

The message was designed to look like a legitimate account-security notification by giving you specific sign-in details and creating a reason to act quickly. The strongest lesson is not whether the location or device looks believable on its own — it is whether you independently verified the activity before trusting the message or following its link.

01
Specific details can create false confidence

The alert supplied a precise location — Tehran, Iran — together with an IP address (185.203.119.27) and platform information (Windows / Chrome). Detailed information can make a message feel authentic, but attackers can include realistic-looking technical details to increase credibility.

02
Urgency is the real trigger

An “unusual sign-in” notification naturally makes people want to secure their account immediately. That pressure can shorten the normal decision process. When a security message creates urgency, slow down rather than allowing the urgency to decide your next action.

03
Verify the event outside the message

Do not use the notification itself as proof that the alert is genuine. Open the known company security portal or trusted account-management application directly and check recent sign-in activity there. This breaks the attacker's link between the message and your decision.

04
Use the reporting path when something feels wrong

If an unexpected sign-in alert cannot be independently verified, report it through the approved Mubadala Energy security reporting process. Reporting early can help the security team investigate the message and protect other users.

The decision to remember: A detailed security alert is not automatically a trustworthy alert. Pause → Verify independently → Report.
